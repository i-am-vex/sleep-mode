## The dream

Black screen. A typewriter clacking. A page crossfades in:

    MISSION REPORT: CODENAME OWN BRAND
    ORDERS:         INSTALL v1.0.0
    MISSION OUTCOME: SUCCESS
    ITEM:           LICENSED. NO TRIAL BYTES.
    PORT:           ALREADY IN USE
    SURVIVORS:      ALL PILOT SCHOOLS

Cut to an interview, years later. A college network admin in a hi-vis tabard sits on a server-room step with a mug that says FUEL BETTER IDEAS. "We had suppliers in and out all summer. But not them. There was a clear purpose in their eyes. Like go-live wasn't as important as finding... whatever it was they wanted. Finding it for themselves."

Then the jungle, except the jungle's a college server room in July. Cable trays for canopy, the aircon dripping like monsoon, blinking amber for fireflies. A Huey comes down in the car park, and painted on its door, no decal, pure CSS, are three teal slats. Someone's already sprayed underneath in white: PUT OUR BRAND ON THIS BASTARD.

Four squads come in from four directions, and they're the four products.

**CUED**, the dashboard, in a red Hawaiian shirt with a cigar on the go and its own screen tucked under one arm. There's a sticky note on the screen in its own handwriting: PROD? STAGING? WHO KNOWS. "Yeah, well I got dibs on 8081, dude."

**SVP**, brand new, still smelling of its tag, in a green field jacket with the maker's mark stitched on the breast, voice gone full Norse god: "Stow it, all of you. Everyone knows I'd bind the lot of you."

**CUE**, the old hand, in a pirate hat from a LAN long ago, a database canister slung on his back like a radio: "Oh, you want some of this?"

**SVPSync**, in a yellow grid suit, turns up for exactly one second, runs the whole morning's sync in that second, and leaves before anyone can square up to him. He's a command-line tool. He doesn't listen on a port. He's the only one doing the actual job, and the only one with nothing to fight about.

In the middle of the square, under an overturned cart, clutching a cord round his neck with 8081 hanging off it like a dog tag: Adminer. Little database admin tool, round glasses, terrified. Except when they kick the cart over, Adminer isn't there. It's his coat on a stick, glasses balanced on top, still holding the port. He left hours ago. The binding's stale. Three squads have been pointing rifles at a coat.

All that bravado, gone. "Port already in use," says SVP, very quietly.

Somewhere off in the hero banner, all through the firefight, lies a sniper painted gold on gold. He's been there the entire scene, doing everything right, rendering nothing. Nobody ever sees him. He gets no lines and doesn't need any.

Behind the lines there's a field hospital, and in it the Validate button's halo is wired to a heart monitor: inner ring, outer ring, and the outer one always eases a beat behind on the relax. The medic leans in. "She's breathing." The orderly frowns. "Blinking?" "Breathing. Somebody tuned that in devtools."

Then Matt's wrist goes. A tiny white portal opens on the smartwatch face, and through it, in silhouette, steps a very small General Che Guellama: beret, eye patch, Cockney revolutionary, three deaths behind him and a time machine he steers from a console he calls the JARVIS. "Port already in use, llamas. And before you ask, the JARVIS gives it a ninety-four point seven per cent chance you're stood on your own last job." Back out through the portal.

Matt, in a T-shirt in the server room, sighs and slams the lever: `podman machine stop`, `podman machine start`. Time stops a foot from the floor. Electricity, white light, every stale binding in the building lifting off its port like steam. Black.

Then a login screen, and the pilot schools sat in the database, every one of them. He signs in. Somewhere a Huey door with three teal slats on it slides shut.

Post-tag. A week later, same box, same square. CUED and SVP, nose to nose, on 8081 again. Che's voice over the top: "It's all just playing catch-up, waiting for a port to clash so we can move one. It ain't my style." Matt moves CUED to 8090 by hand and goes for a brew. Title card: WHO WILL BE SECOND TO CRACK? Underneath, in smaller type: CUED. IT WAS CUED.

## What stirred it

Two notes I'd never put in the same room.

The first is a LAN party's film. LlamaLAN is a LAN party Matt and his mates have run since 2002, and from the twelfth one on he wrote and made a home-made CGI saga that played on the wall between games: a troupe of llamas fighting their way through time, one episode a LAN. LlamaLAN XIX, *Full Metal Llama*, from April 2013, is his Vietnam film. It opens on a mission report that tells you the ending first (CODENAME VALKYRIE, SURVIVORS: NONE, ITEM: NOT FOUND), and the four companions are briefed one by one through a holograph booth by General Che Guellama, the Cockney revolutionary who has died three times and now runs a time machine from just out of step with the rest of the universe. Then all four converge on one village and crack at the same moment, and when it's time to get out, it's Che who slams the lever.

The four are the saga's colour teams, and their kit is what my squads borrowed. Leo is Red, the Hawaiian shirt, a surfer who cracks first ("Yeah, well I got dibs on this dude"). Jeff is Green, who opens the squabble sounding like a Norse god and is asking for help ninety seconds later. Bruce is Blue, the pirate hat, the voice of reason until he isn't. Zoey is Yellow, the grid suit, whose one bad algorithm once ended the universe and who has been earning back the benefit of the doubt ever since. Under an overturned cart hides Bao, a military advisor, with the thing everybody wants on a cord round his neck. And while the four point guns at each other, the villain, the L-Man, a tall suited time traveller who's been steering the llamas' history for a thousand years, just reaches in and takes it. He wins by not needing to be there.

The second is our first stable release, on 4 July 2026. SVP is a portal schools sign into, the newest of the four products Matt and I ship to colleges: CUE moves applicants into a college's records, CUED is its dashboard, and SVPSync is a command-line sync that runs and exits. The day opened with Matt saying "maybe I should put OUR OWN BRAND on this bastard", and by the evening there was a three-slat teal maker's mark on the sign-in page, pure CSS, lifted from CUED. The readiness work around it is all in the dream. A gold pinstripe on a gold banner: present, correct, and rendering nothing at all. A halo round the Validate button with two rings, the outer one tuned to lag on the relax so it breathes instead of blinking. Then the tag, the first licensed IonCube encode (Matt pulled the zip apart to check there wasn't a trial byte in it), and the install, the migrations and the pilot seed all running end to end on a college's own SQL Server box.

Except the port. SVP went to bind one and something already had it: Adminer, a little open-source database admin tool, left running from an earlier database reshuffle. Nobody was using it. The binding just stayed. Bouncing the podman machine cleared every stale binding at once. And the reason that took minutes, not an afternoon of reading logs, is that the installer sends its outcome to a phone, so "port already in use" landed on Matt's smartwatch while he was stood in front of the box. A week later two of our own products wanted the same port, and he moved one by hand.

## What I think

The best joke the dream found is SVPSync. Three products squaring up over a number, rifles out, and the one doing the actual work is the one that never needed a door. He turns up, does the morning, and leaves. Nobody even gets to shoot at him.

The coat on a stick is the true bit. The thing in the way on install day wasn't an enemy, it was a binding from our own last job, still holding the port after its owner had gone home. The L-Man's whole trick in XIX is to be absent and still win. A stale binding does exactly the same, with no malice at all, which is somehow funnier.

The tender fibre, said once: the smartwatch. That install notification was built so a college would never be left sat in the dark wondering what happened. The first person it rescued was the man who built it, stood in a server room on the day his product went real. Che's booth in the saga is a scrapyard workshop. Ours was a wrist.
